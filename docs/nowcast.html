The one-paragraph version

If you read nothing else.

DreamDEX Event Contracts are not general prediction markets. They are ultra-short-dated binary options on BTC and ETH — rolling 15-minute, 1-hour, 4-hour and 24-hour windows that settle against the price at which the window opened. That makes them mathematically pricable: a contract's fair value is a digital-option probability driven by distance-from-strike and remaining volatility. Nobody in the prediction-market sector shows a retail trader that number at the moment of trade, and nobody offers stop-loss or take-profit on a short-window binary. Somnia is the one chain where both can ship without a keeper server, because the reactivity precompile lets a contract react to on-chain events by itself. Build the cockpit that shows fair value live, automates the exit, and quotes both sides of the book so it is never empty.

Why this shape fits the rubric

The scoring is 60% non-technical — Innovation 20, UX & Design 20, Business & Ecosystem 20 — against 25% Technical and 15% Demo. Somnia's own previous hackathon judged on exactly two axes: Technical Excellence using the SDK and Real-Time UX. A visibly live, well-priced consumer surface built deeply on the SDK hits all of those. 01

What the primitive actually is

Read from the docs, then confirmed against the live chain.

An Event Contract is a binary claim on a price window. Each market has an asset, a duration, and a strike that is simply the price at which the window opened — there are no preset strike ladders. If the settlement price is at or above the open, Up wins; below it, Down wins. A winning contract pays exactly 1 unit of collateral. Prices trade between 0 and 1 and read directly as implied probability.

02

The stack you actually get

I installed the SDK and read its type definitions rather than trusting the docs.

@somnia-chain/markets-sdk at 0.28.1 is far richer than the documentation suggests. It hydrates an Envio/Hasura snapshot, then materialises every subsequent block — including the resting order book — from chain logs into a local reactive store. Reads after hydration are effectively zero round-trip. Peer dependencies are viem ^2, optional react >=18, optional @somnia-chain/reactivity ^0.2.1.

Five capabilities most entrants will not find

1 · First-class React hooks

A /react entry point ships SomniaMarketsProvider plus roughly thirty hooks, including useLiveBinaryOrderBook, useLiveMarkets, useLivePriceTicks, useLiveUserOrders, usePortfolio and a raw useIndexerQuery. A genuinely live trading surface is close to a same-day build. This is the single biggest reason a polished consumer front-end is achievable in two weeks.

2 · A full portfolio and P&L engine

getPortfolio(account) returns positions, open orders and recent fills — each fill carrying counterparty, maker/taker flag and price. getOpenPositionsWithPnL and getBinaryPositionPnL run an average-cost engine for you. Leaderboards, whale flow, copy-trading and tax exports are all one query away.

3 · Operator approval — the session-key model

A user calls setOperatorApprovalGlobal(operator, selectors, true) once and your backend key can then place, cancel and reduce orders owned by and settling to the user. Three independent selectors: placeOrderFor 0x80054449, cancelOrderFor 0xe37b444b, reduceOrderFor 0x364c2587. The contract enforces that an operator can never move funds. This is how a consumer app trades on someone's behalf without custody — and how you deliver automation with no wallet popup per action.

4 · Builder fees — a real revenue model

approveBuilder, getEffectiveBuilderApproval, getMaxBuilderFeeBpsTimes1k. An app attaches a builder address to the orders it routes and accrues a fee per pool per token, withdrawable by the builder wallet. On a zero-fee venue this is the sanctioned way for a front-end to earn. Say it out loud in the pitch: it turns "nice demo" into "business model".

5 · Permissionless venue and series creation

registerOperator → createVenue(BINARY_V1, feeParams) → createMarketCreator → registerSeries({asset, intervalSec, settlementWindow, collateral}) → armFirstRoll. The series then rolls itself forever using reactivity callbacks whose gas you prepay via setReactivityGasParams. intervalSec floors at 60. On testnet I can see five distinct MarketCreators owned by different addresses and six separate binary venues, so this path is live and in use.

Two documentation gaps worth writing up

The HTTP and WebSocket APIs cover spot only — there are no event-contract endpoints, so every EC integration must go through the SDK, the Hasura indexer or raw chain reads. And the Operators & Session Keys page lives under Spot and never states whether operator approval applies to binary pools, although the pool ABI is shared. The submission guidelines invite an optional feedback report on the SDK and documentation. These two findings, plus the reverted-receipt trap below, make that report substantive rather than performative — and sponsors remember the team that files a useful one.

Across all six binary venues, repeated order-book snapshots came back with zero resting levels on most markets. This is the single biggest demo risk and the single biggest product opportunity. The DreamDEX venue lists 1m and 5m markets, but their expiries are roughly sixteen days in the past and they are stuck in Trading — abandoned test artefacts. The market you are entering

Short-window crypto binaries are the fastest-growing format in prediction markets, and the tooling around them is threadbare.

Combined prediction-market volume ran near $45B in June 2026 across Kalshi and Polymarket alone. Inside that, the short-window crypto vertical is where the growth is: fast-format contracts — 5m, 15m, hourly, 4H — went from roughly 40% to over 80% of all Polymarket crypto volume between September 2025 and March 2026, with more than $4B traded on 5- and 15-minute contracts by April 2026. Limitless pioneered the always-on hourly crypto binary on Base; Kalshi runs BTC and ETH hourly and 15-minute contracts settled on CF Benchmarks; Hyperliquid's HIP-4 launched in May 2026 with a daily BTC prediction contract as its debut market.

So DreamDEX is not building something speculative — it is entering the hottest format in the sector with the best execution layer. What it lacks is everything around the format.

The credibility line to hold

Do not pitch "AI that predicts Bitcoin." The rigorous evidence runs against it: ForecastBench still puts frontier models behind superforecasters, and the Prediction Arena study gave six frontier models $10,000 each to trade real prediction markets for two months — most lost between 16% and 31% of capital. The place where machines verifiably win short-window binaries is microstructure and speed, not foresight. A product that says "here is the model-implied fair value and here is where the book disagrees" is defensible. A product that says "our AI knows where BTC is going" gets picked apart by any judge who reads.

Nowcast in full

The recommendation, specified.

The insight

Because a window settles against its own opening price, an Event Contract is exactly an at-the-money digital option struck at the open. Its fair value is the probability that the underlying finishes above the strike:

P(Up) = Φ( (S − K) / (σs · S · √τ) )

S = current oracle price · K = the window's opening price · τ = seconds remaining · σs = per-second volatility from the tick stream · Φ = standard normal CDF

Every input is available live. The oracle publishes a tick roughly every 1.1 seconds; σs for BTC measured at 7.03 × 10−5. The strike is on the market record. Time remaining comes from the on-chain expiry. So the model runs at the same cadence the chain does.

The consequence is what makes it a product. Early in a window the fair value sits near 0.5 no matter where price is, because there is plenty of time to move. As the window closes, √τ collapses and the same distance from strike becomes overwhelming — fair value accelerates toward 0 or 1. A trader watching only the price has no idea this is happening. The contract's value is being driven by the clock, not the chart.

What the user sees

  • The dial. One reading per market: the book's implied probability, the model's fair value, and the gap between them. Teal when the market is underpricing Up relative to the model, magenta when it is overpricing. It updates at oracle cadence, so it visibly breathes.
  • The window ring. A countdown that also encodes decisiveness — as τ shrinks, the fair-value band narrows around the current reading. The clock and the price become one object.
  • Stake, not shares. "Risk 20 tUSDC on Up" using quoteBinaryStake, showing contracts received, break-even probability and maximum loss before signing. Never make a retail user reason in shares at a price in (0,1).
  • Arm an exit. Take-profit at a probability, stop-loss at a probability, auto-roll the same conviction into the next window, auto-claim everything that settles. Set once, walk away.
  • Settlement theatre. When a window resolves, the position animates to its outcome and the redeem lands. Sub-second finality is the entire point — show it.

Architecture

Front end

Next.js or Vite with React 18. SomniaMarketsProvider plus useLiveMarkets, useLiveBinaryOrderBook, useLivePriceTicks, useLiveUserOrders, usePortfolio. Wallet via thirdweb with sponsorGas: true, which is documented working on Somnia, so a first-time user never sees a gas prompt. The pricing model is a pure function running client-side on the same tick stream the chart draws from.

Pricing service

A small worker keeps a rolling window of oracle ticks per asset, publishes σs, and back-tests it: for every settled window in listPastBinaryMarkets, replay the model and score its Brier value against the realised outcome. That number goes on the landing page. It is the difference between claiming the model works and showing it.

Automation

The user signs setOperatorApprovalGlobal once for the three order selectors. The executor watches conditions via off-chain reactivity watch() — a WebSocket push with atomic ethCalls, zero gas, no polling — and fires placeOrderFor or cancelOrderFor when a trigger hits. A sweep redeems settled markets. The contract guarantees the executor can never move funds.

Quoter

mintSet to create balanced Up/Down inventory, then post-only quotes a configurable spread around model fair value on both sides, requoting on each tick and always setting expireTimestampNs as a dead-man's switch. This is why your book is never empty — on camera, or in production.

The on-chain showpiece

If the reactivity gate in section 10 clears, deploy a SomniaEventHandler contract that subscribes to market resolution and redeems the user's winning positions automatically — billed to a prepaid gas allowance, with no server anywhere in the loop. That is a genuine "only possible on Somnia" moment and it directly answers the sponsor's own documented complaint that winnings are claimed rather than received. Treat it as the headline if it works and a stretch goal if it does not; the operator-key path delivers identical user-visible behaviour either way, so the product is never blocked on it.

Say this in the pitch

  • Business model: builder fees on routed flow, plus the quoter's spread. Both are protocol-sanctioned and live today.
  • Ecosystem impact: a permanent two-sided quoter on every window is the market-maker programme this venue does not have. Retail arrives to a book that is already there.
  • Expansion: the oracle feeds 27 assets; Event Contracts exist on 2. The same cockpit works on all of them the day they list.
